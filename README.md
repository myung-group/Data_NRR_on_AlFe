This repository accompanies the paper " **Spontaneous Nitrate-to-Ammonia Conversion Driven by Aluminum Corrosion at Fe Impurity Sites** " by Seon Young Hwang, Gi Beom Sim, Ju Young Maeng, Gaeun Yun, Choong Kyun Rhee, Sunghak Park, Soohaeng Yoo Willow, Chang Woo Myung, Youngku Sohn.
* ```Data/``` contains inputs and outputs of 1) geometry relaxation and 2) vibrational frequency calculation of nitrate reduction reaction intermediates.
  * Calculated data file ```NRR_on_AlFe.xlsx``` for Gibbs free energy exists in ```Data/```.

* ```Figures/``` contains figures of the paper that obtained from ```Data/```.
* ```Scripts/``` contains Jupyter notebook and Python scripts that can reproduce figures.
  * Zero-point energy, entropy, and enthalpy corrections can be calculated using the Python scripts ```Scripts/calculate_*.py``` at the directory ```Data/geometry_relaxation_and_zpe/.../zpe/```

## Paper abstract
Zero-valent aluminum (Al) has long been used as a sacrificial reductant for nitrate, yet what enables it to convert nitrate to ammonia has remained unknown. Here we show that this activity does not originate from Al itself but from trace Fe impurities embedded in commercial Al. Under open-circuit conditions in N2-saturated 0.2 M KOH + 0.02 M KNO3, commercial Al (99.6%) produced NH3 at 8.4 ± 0.5 µmol cm−2 h−1 and directed 36% of the product-bound electrons to NH3, whereas ultrapure Al (99.999%) yielded eight-fold less NH3 (electron selectivity 11%) and standalone Fe, Co, Ni, and Zn foils were inactive. Selective corrosion of the Al matrix enriched surface Fe about 50-fold (0.20 → 10.3 at.%) as metallic Fe cores with oxidized shells, and only under NH3-producing conditions. NH3 formation follows Langmuir–Hinshelwood kinetics in nitrate, which inhibits H2 evolution with an inverse first-order dependence, and the apparent activation energy of NH3 formation (23.0 kJ mol−1) is similar to that of Al dissolution (22.5 kJ mol−1) but half that of H2 evolution (44.1 kJ mol−1). Density functional theory calculations that include the Al oxidation driving force show that Fe in the Al(111) surface lowers the barriers for removing O-containing intermediates and thus mitigates the oxygen poisoning that limits pure Al. Trace impurities therefore act not as passive contaminants but as the catalytic sites of a self-sustaining micro-galvanic Al–Fe couple, a mechanism that must be considered both in designing corrosion-driven nitrogen conversion and in benchmarking nitrate electrocatalysts.
